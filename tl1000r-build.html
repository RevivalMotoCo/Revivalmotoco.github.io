The Motorcycle

Why The TL1000R Matters

Launched in 1998, the TL1000R was Suzuki's fully-faired, race-focused development of the TL1000S and its answer to the big V-twin superbikes of the period. At its heart is a 996cc, liquid-cooled 90-degree V-twin.

Suzuki intended the TL-R for superbike competition, but the racing programme never developed as planned. Production lasted from 1998 to 2003. Its unmistakable shape, booming V-twin and unusual engineering have since given it a cult following.

And they are genuinely uncommon. Contemporary UK reporting puts total UK TL1000R sales at around 2,300 motorcycles. More than two decades after production ended, complete, relatively original examples are increasingly difficult to find.

This one isn't being turned into something it never was. The aim is to understand it, preserve what is worth preserving, rectify what needs attention and put it back on the road properly.

Stage 01 · At RMC

Initial Assessment

The yellow TL1000R arrived as a running, complete motorcycle. Before ordering our way out of problems, the first job is to understand the whole bike: condition, originality, wear, leaks, consumables and the evidence left by previous maintenance.

Stage 02 · Strip-Down

Getting Under The Bodywork

With the bodywork removed, the chassis, running gear, wiring, cooling system and normally hidden areas can be inspected properly. This is where a recommissioning becomes more than a service and a polish.

Stage 03 · Going Deeper

The Bits You Don't Normally See

With the TL opened up further, we can get into the areas that disappear once the bodywork is back on: frame and swingarm sections, cooling components, hoses and connections, wiring, braking hardware and the accumulated grime that can hide developing problems.

These aren't glamour photographs. They're the useful ones — a record of the condition we found and the level of inspection taking place before the bike goes back together.
