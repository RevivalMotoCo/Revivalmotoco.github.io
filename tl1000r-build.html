RMC Build Journal

Suzuki
TL1000R

From arrival to ready for the road. This is the running record of what we find, what we do and how the first RMC recommission progresses.

Recommissioning Underway
Stage 01 · At RMC

Initial Assessment

The yellow TL1000R is the first motorcycle through the RMC workshop. The aim is to understand the whole bike properly rather than simply fix the obvious items.

The bike arrived as a running, complete motorcycle. Inspection, cleaning and methodical strip-down let us see what years of use have left behind.

Stage 02 · Strip-Down

Getting Under The Bodywork

Bodywork has been removed and the TL is opened up for a proper look at the frame, engine, gearbox, suspension, brakes, wiring and cooling system.

This is the point of the RMC process: find the issues now, while the motorcycle is in the workshop, rather than after it has been advertised.

Next

Inspection, servicing and recommissioning continue. We'll add the work, findings and photographs here as the TL progresses.

Stage 03 · Going Deeper

The Bits You Don't Normally See

With the TL opened up further, we can inspect the frame, engine, hoses, wiring, brakes and components normally hidden beneath the bodywork. These photographs record the condition we found before cleaning and repairs.

Stage 04 · Workshop Update

Cleaning Reveals The Real Work

The engine is cleaning up well, but the throttle bodies have substantial surface corrosion and need careful inspection. We've removed the throttle-body assembly to assess its seals and linkages before cleaning. The radiators and cooling system are also being checked.

The wheels have been stripped of their discs and sprocket components. We're considering refinishing options and will document the outcome once the work is complete.

Stage 05 · Initial Findings

Behind The Front Sprocket Cover

Removing the front sprocket cover revealed a substantial build-up of old chain lubricant, road dirt and oily residue around the sprocket and gear-selector shaft. We need to clean the area and inspect the relevant seals before determining whether any oil leak is present.

Stage 06 · Cleaning & Inspection

What Lies Beneath

Here's why we document the less glamorous jobs. Cleaning the front sprocket area has exposed the components previously hidden beneath thick grease and road dirt. That makes it easier to inspect the gear-selector shaft, nearby seals and surrounding engine cases.

Engine And Front End

We're working through the exposed engine, cleaning accessible surfaces and inspecting hoses, connections and fixings. Cleaning is progress, not proof of a completed repair; any suspected leaks will be checked before reassembly.

Still To Come

Seal inspection, servicing, outstanding mechanical work, reassembly, road test and MOT preparation.

Stage 07 · Fuel & Induction

Twenty-Eight Years Beneath The Surface

The throttle bodies are off the motorcycle for a closer look at the throttle-position sensor, linkages, return springs and electrical connectors. Surface corrosion and accumulated dirt are particularly evident around the operating mechanism. We are documenting their condition while the assembly is cleaned and checked, rather than claiming the job is finished.

Inside The Fuel Tank

We've removed the fuel-pump assembly from the yellow tank to inspect the mounting flange, pump components, wiring and sealing surfaces. The photographs show corrosion and deterioration on the assembly and residue around the tank opening. The tank-to-pump gasket is being addressed as part of the recommissioning.

Spark Plug Inspection

Old spark plugs are being checked as part of the service. The comparison photograph records their condition before replacement; a photograph alone cannot establish the engine's internal condition.

Next

Complete the fuel-system work, verify the electrical connections and throttle operation, and check for leaks before refitting the tank and bodywork.

To Follow

Repairs · Reassembly · Road Test

This journal will grow with the motorcycle, documenting the work completed, reassembly, road testing, MOT preparation and the finished bike before it is offered for sale.
